Where the pixels went

The spec overruns the panel by 128px

Your fixed elements are 88px of header, a 300px hero, and a 112px bar. That is 500px, leaving exactly 300px. Two rows of 160px tiles plus the section label and its gaps needs 428px. There is no arrangement in which all the stated numbers coexist inside 800px — so, per your rule, I cut rather than scrolled. Both cuts are to container heights; no content was dropped.

Decisions & caveats

What to check before this ships

Tile icons are 52px, not 64

A 115px tile cannot hold a 64px icon plus a 24px Label with any padding left over — that needs 96px of the 115 and leaves 9px top and bottom. 52px keeps the proportion honest. If the 64px icon matters more than the second row, drop to four categories and put the rest behind Browse.

Tiles are 278 wide, not 240

Four 240px tiles with 24px gutters measure 1032, which floats inside the 1184px content width and leaves the grid misaligned with the hero above it. I widened them to fill the measure so both edges line up. One measure reads better than two.

Display at 56px caps hero titles at ~16 characters

The text side is 45% of 1184, less padding — about 477px. "Mahad Satyagraha" fits on one line with almost nothing spare. Anything longer wraps to two lines and the hero overflows. Whoever writes the featured item needs that budget in front of them, or the field needs a hard character limit.

The idle rail is legible, and that's a choice

8px of saturated brass across the full width is not subtle — it reads as a progress bar, because it is one. If it should be near-invisible until the last ten seconds, drop it to 3px at 25% opacity and have it fade up as the timer runs down. Your call; the spec said 8px, so it is 8px.

Any touch restarts the count

The rail resets on any interaction inside the frame, not just on navigation. Reading a long caption without touching anything is exactly the case where a visitor gets reset mid-sentence — consider pausing the timer on detail screens rather than running it everywhere.

Hero art is a stand-in

The crowd-at-the-tank plate is drawn, not a scan. It exists to test the 55/45 split and the right-edge gradient that keeps the Display type off a busy image. Replace with the real photograph and re-check that gradient — a light sky at the seam will need it stronger.
